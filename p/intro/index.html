Tell me about yourself

That wasn't you.
That was your biodata.

Name, native place, father's job, hobbies. Every fresher says it, so every interviewer forgets it. Here's the 60-second answer that actually sticks, and the prompt that writes yours.

What most people say

"My name is Maddie. I was born in Hyderabad. My father is a businessman, my mother is a homemaker. My hobbies are cricket and sleeping. My strength is I'm a hard worker."

What gets remembered

"I'm a computer science fresher who builds data tools. In my final year I built an app that cut report time from two hours to ten minutes. I want to do that at scale, and that's why this role."

Three parts. Sixty seconds.

Nothing about your family, your hobbies or "hard worker" unless the role asks for it.

Now ~15 sec

Who you are today, in one line: your degree plus what you do or build.

2

Proof ~30 sec

One real thing you built or did, with a number: time saved, users, accuracy, marks, team size.

Next ~15 sec

Why this role, at this company. Connect your proof to what they need.

You are a friendly senior interviewer at an Indian tech company. I am a fresher preparing for the question "Tell me about yourself" for the job below. Help me build a 60-second answer that is 100% true.

Step 1 - Ask first: read my resume and the job post. Then ask me up to 5 short questions to find my strongest real story (a project, internship, college work or result) and the real numbers in it (time saved, users, rows of data, accuracy, marks, team size). Wait for my answers before writing anything.

Step 2 - Write my answer in 3 parts, about 130-150 words, in simple spoken English:
- NOW (1-2 lines): who I am today - my degree and what I do or build. No name, native place, family details or hobbies.
- PROOF (2-3 lines): my single best real story, with the number.
- NEXT (1-2 lines): why this exact role at this company, linked to my proof. Use the job title from the post.

Step 3 - Make it sound like me: short sentences, no big words like "passionate", "synergy" or "hardworking individual". It should sound natural said out loud, not read from paper.

Step 4 - Give me 2 versions: a 60-second one and a 30-second one (for when they say "keep it short").

Step 5 - Follow-ups: list the 3 questions the interviewer will most likely ask right after my answer, with short honest answers based only on my real experience.

Step 6 - Practice tips: tell me which 3 words to stress, where to pause, and one thing to avoid saying.

Strict rule: NEVER invent any skill, project, internship, company or number I don't have. If my story is weak, tell me honestly and suggest a small real project I can finish in one week to make it stronger.

Before the interview

  1. Say it out loud 5 times. Record it on your phone once and listen back.
  2. Don't memorise word for word. Remember the three parts and the number. The rest comes naturally.
  3. Change "Next" for every company. Read their job post and use their words.

Three rules

  • Only true things. They will ask about every line. A small real project beats a big fake one.
  • One story, not five. A single clear example with a number is remembered. A list of skills isn't.
  • Stop at 60 seconds. End on "Next" and let them ask the next question.
