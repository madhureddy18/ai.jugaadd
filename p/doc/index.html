How we make a 3D AI reel in code.

No camera, no editing app. An AI (Claude) writes the script and the 3D scenes, a free voice app makes the voices, and you review and approve. A 36-second reel takes about 3 hours, half of it the render.

Step 1 · Idea and script

  1. Pick the idea. One relatable moment or one useful thing (a prompt, a tool, a trick). Reels that give something away for free get saved and shared most.
  2. Write the hook first. The first 2 seconds must say what's coming. A hook that hides the point loses people.
  3. Write the lines. One character asks or reacts, the AI sidekick answers short and dry. 12–23 lines, 30–40 seconds.
  4. Mark the emotions. [sigh], [laughter], surprise, a question tone. Your characters have tiny faces, so the voice carries the feeling.
  5. Mark caption keywords so they show in yellow.
  6. End with a one-word CTA. "Comment DOC". Never a sentence for people to type.
  7. Review, then say "build".
Write a 35-second Instagram reel script. Two characters: [main character] and a dry, funny AI sidekick. Topic: [topic]. The hook must reveal the premise in the first line. 15–20 short lines, mark emotions like [sigh] [laughter] [surprise] on lines that need them, end with "Comment [WORD]". Make it genuinely funny.

Step 2 · Characters and look

  1. Build the characters once in code with three.js (a free JavaScript 3D library) and reuse them in every reel.
  2. Rules that make them look right: shoulders, arms and legs connected to the body; small lip movement when talking; no blush dots.
  3. The set: a room built from simple shapes. Change the location when the story needs it.
  4. Acting: poses per line (look at the sidekick, lean in, both arms up) and a camera shot per beat.
  5. Screen rules: full 9:16, no black bars, captions high on screen.
Using three.js, build a cute toy-style 3D character sitting on a chair: rounded head, connected shoulders/arms/legs, simple eyes, a mouth that opens with a talk value 0–1, and functions to set poses (rest, wave, both arms up, hand on chin). Keep it all in code, no model files.

The same 3D scene is redrawn by a shader (a small program that recolors every pixel), so a new look costs code, not new models. Match the style to the line's emotion, and cut on the line where it changes.

Step 4 · Voices

🎙OmniVoice StudioFree, open source, runs on your computer. No API key.
  1. Install OmniVoice Studio from its GitHub page and open it.
  2. Design each voice once. In Voice Design set gender, age, accent and pitch ("male, teenager, moderate pitch, American accent"), generate one sample and save it.
  3. Clone from that saved sample every time so your characters sound the same in every reel.
  4. One file per line, named in order (01.wav, 02.wav…). Add emotion where the script marks it.
  5. Clean up: trim silence at both ends. Add a style effect where it fits: 8-bit crunch for pixel art, echo for neon.
  6. Listen and fix: regenerate any line that sounds rushed or flat. 20 lines take about 15 minutes.

Step 5 · Building the scene

  1. Timeline from the voices: each clip's real length sets when every line starts, so cuts land on words.
  2. Shots: one camera shot per beat, cut where the mood changes.
  3. Motion graphics: titles, step badges, counters, stickers drawn on top of the 3D.
  4. Captions high on screen, keyword in yellow.
  5. Sound: pops and whooshes on cuts, music that dips under the big line.

Step 6 · Render

  1. Check a few still frames first at the busiest moments.
  2. Full render: every frame at 1080×1920, 30 fps, drawn in headless Chrome, joined with voices and sound by ffmpeg. 36 seconds ≈ 1,100 frames ≈ 1.5 hours.
  3. Changing only the voices still needs a full render: new clip lengths move every cut, caption and mouth.

Step 7 · Post

  1. Watch it once on your phone. Sound levels, captions readable.
  2. Cover: the frame with the hook title.
  3. Caption: hook, a short list, then the one-word CTA.
  4. 3–5 specific hashtags, not 30 generic ones.
  5. Post when your audience is online. Midday and 7–9 PM work well in India.
  6. Deliver the freebie to everyone who comments the word.

8Talk to your PDF. "300-page textbook. One question." → PDF
9Cold message that gets replies. "Why does nobody reply to my DMs?" → REPLY
10Real or AI? "Guess which one is AI." → GUESS

Keep it under 40 seconds, show the result before explaining it, and make the giveaway something people would save.
